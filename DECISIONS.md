# Platform-MQ Decisions and History

Context behind architectural choices, known gotchas, and outstanding work. This document captures institutional knowledge that isn't derivable from the code or configs alone.

## Why MSK?

ConsoleDot's Kafka infrastructure has been through three eras:

1. **Self-hosted Strimzi** — Kafka was originally deployed via the Strimzi operator on OpenShift. This required significant planning and political effort to get deployed (elevated access, operator approvals, credential management). Once running, the operational burden was substantial: Zookeeper cluster issues, topic management, Connect/connector maintenance, and ongoing user education about Kafka behavior (particularly around consumer lag expectations). It consumed a disproportionate amount of time relative to the team's other responsibilities.

2. **Red Hat OpenShift Streams for Apache Kafka (RHOSAK)** — When Red Hat developed a managed Kafka product, ConsoleDot was brought on as an early internal user. The arrangement was meant to be mutually beneficial: reduced operational burden for us, real-world validation for RHOSAK. Our workload exceeded what the platform could handle, despite not being particularly large by Kafka standards. The product was eventually discontinued.

3. **Amazon MSK (current)** — The app-sre team built Terraform/qontract integration to provision MSK clusters via app-interface. We migrated immediately. The effort to plan and execute the data migration was significant, but the result was roughly an 80% reduction in Kafka-related operational work. AWS handles connectivity, security updates, storage management, and broker infrastructure.

## Why a Standalone Topic Operator?

App-interface does not currently support direct topic management against MSK. Rather than managing topics manually or building custom tooling, the Strimzi standalone topic operator was retained as a lightweight bridge — it watches KafkaTopic CRDs in OpenShift and reconciles them against the MSK brokers. This was the lowest-lift path to keep declarative topic management working after the MSK migration.

If app-interface adds native topic management support in the future, the standalone operator could, and should, be retired.

## Why Helm for Topic Templates?

Originally topics were generated by a custom script. A team member contributed a Helm chart that templated KafkaTopic resources from a simple values.yaml, and a GitHub Action automates the generation on PR. What started as an unexpected contribution turned out to be a clean, maintainable approach: topic properties are defined declaratively in `helm/kafka-topics/values.yaml`, and the generated output in `deploys/openshift/kafka-topics.yaml` is exactly what the standalone topic operator expects.

## Known Gotchas

### ACLs — The Cluster Resource Rule

**DO NOT set ALLOW ALL on cluster resources. It will prevent inter-broker communication.** Never set any ACL with `--cluster --operation ClusterAction`.

ACLs for public TLS connections are required and cannot be created through the MSK UI — they require CLI commands from an in-cluster debug pod with Kafka binaries. Current method uses Zookeeper connection strings (see example below), but this will change after the KRaft migration.

Example (stage, from a debug pod):
```bash
bin/kafka-acls.sh \
  --authorizer-properties zookeeper.connect=z-3.consoledotstagetesting.ox06qd.c5.kafka.us-east-1.amazonaws.com:2181,z-1.consoledotstagetesting.ox06qd.c5.kafka.us-east-1.amazonaws.com:2181,z-2.consoledotstagetesting.ox06qd.c5.kafka.us-east-1.amazonaws.com:2181 \
  --add \
  --allow-principal "User:<username>" \
  --operation [Read|Write|Alter] \
  --topic '<topic-name>'
```

> **Note:** After the pending KRaft migration, ACL management will likely move to bootstrap-based commands rather than Zookeeper-based. The above syntax will need updating.

### Kafka Connect — Connector Inventory

Two connectors are deployed:

| Connector | Owner/Consumer | Status | Notes |
|-----------|---------------|--------|-------|
| **Confluent S3 Sink** | Data warehouse team | Active | Drops configured topic contents to S3 as JSON. Multiple attempts to migrate to Camel or other S3 sink connectors have been unsuccessful — treat this as a locked-in dependency for now. |
| **Debezium** | RBAC/Kessel team | Legacy | The team has largely moved to their own Connect setup. Maintain for backwards compatibility — update on CVEs but don't invest in new development. |

### Topic Naming

The soft convention is `platform.<service>.<purpose>` (e.g., `platform.notifications.ingress`, `platform.rhsm-subscriptions.contract-sync`). This is a social norm, not an enforced rule — there's no validation or rejection based on naming.

### Consumer Lag Inquiries

Teams frequently report consumer lag as a critical issue. In the vast majority of cases, the cause is client-side (pod restarts, consumer rebalances, misconfigured consumer groups) rather than broker-side. See [OPERATIONS.md](OPERATIONS.md) for the diagnostic playbook.

## Outstanding Future Work

Two significant tasks remain that will fall to the new owning team:

1. **Kafka/KRaft migration** — Upgrade to Kafka 3.x and migrate from Zookeeper to KRaft. This will change how ACLs are managed (moving from Zookeeper-based to bootstrap-based commands) and may affect the standalone topic operator's connection configuration.

2. **MSK Connect migration** — Move from the self-managed Kafka Connect instance (currently built and deployed via Konflux as a custom image) to app-interface-defined, MSK-hosted Connect. This would eliminate the need to maintain the Connect Dockerfile, the Confluent S3 plugin bundle, and the Konflux build pipeline for Connect images.
